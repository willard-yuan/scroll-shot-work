**To take a long screenshot on iPhone Duo, use Safari's Full Page option for webpages, or use ScrollShot to combine a scrolling screen recording or overlapping screenshots into one image.** Safari is the simplest place to start; ScrollShot gives you a practical workflow for chats, app content, and pages that need more control.

Apple's newly released **iPhone Duo** is its first foldable iPhone, with an outer display and a larger inner display. That extra space is useful for reading, but a conversation or article can still extend far beyond the screen. [Apple's iPhone Duo announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-unveils-iphone-duo/) describes the new design.

This guide covers both methods, explains which one to choose, and shows how to avoid repeated text, missing sections, and awkward joins.

## Which long screenshot method should you use?

A regular screenshot captures the current view. A long screenshot preserves more of the content in one continuous result, so readers do not have to open several images and guess their order.

| What you want to capture | Start with | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A complete article or webpage in Safari | Safari Full Page | Built into iPhone and quick to use |
| A chat or scrollable app screen | ScrollShot Auto Stitch | Builds a long image from recorded scrolling |
| Several screenshots you already have | ScrollShot Manual Stitch | Combines overlapping captures in sequence |
| A result with joins that need adjustment | ScrollShot | Offers seam fine-tuning before saving |

For example, use Safari to save a travel article. Use ScrollShot when you need to send the complete conversation about that trip, including messages below the visible screen.

## Method 1: Take a full-page screenshot in Safari

Start here when the content is a webpage. Open the actual page in Safari, let its images load, and dismiss menus or banners that cover the text. If a link opened inside another app, move it into Safari before trying this workflow.

![Safari full-page screenshot workflow: open a webpage, capture the screen, choose Full Page, preview and crop, then save](/how-to-take-a-long-screenshot-on-iphone-duo/safari-full-page-steps.svg)

*Workflow illustration; the exact editor controls can vary with your iOS version and screenshot settings.*

### Capture and save the page

1. **Open the webpage in Safari.** Navigate to the content you want to keep.
2. **Press and quickly release the side button and volume-up button together.**
3. **Open the screenshot editor.** Tap the thumbnail if one appears; depending on your settings, a full-screen preview may open directly.
4. **Choose Full Page.** Use the preview on the right to inspect the capture, and crop it if needed.
5. **Save the result.** Open the save controls, then choose the available image or PDF option.

These capture and preview steps follow [Apple's current iPhone screenshot guide](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/iphone/-iphc872c0115/ios).

Choose **Save to Photos** when available for an image, or **Save PDF to Files** for a PDF. Apple documents both options in its [full-page screenshot instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/iphone/iphc872c0115/18.0/ios/18.0), so older advice that Safari captures are always PDF-only is incomplete. Labels and availability can vary by software version and capture.

### When Safari is enough—and when to switch

Safari works well for a straightforward page you want to read later. Before sharing, check the beginning, middle, and end: a long-looking preview is not proof that every section you wanted is included.

If **Full Page is missing**, try a normal article opened directly in Safari. This helps distinguish a page-specific problem from a broader issue. For an endlessly loading feed, a chat, or content whose preview is incomplete, move to ScrollShot and capture the specific section you need.

### Option A: Record your scrolling and stitch automatically

This is useful for a long conversation, a sequence of app screens in one scrollable view, or a webpage that Safari does not capture as expected.

1. **Prepare the content.** Find the beginning of the section you need. Close the keyboard and dismiss pop-ups so they do not cover the page.
2. **Open ScrollShot and select Auto Stitch.** Tap **Start System Recording**, then follow the iOS recording prompt for ScrollShot.
3. **Switch to the content and scroll steadily.** Move through it in one direction. Pause briefly where images need time to load, and avoid jumping between unrelated pages.
4. **Stop recording at the end.** Use the system recording control. Return to ScrollShot, or open its recording-complete notification if enabled.
5. **Keep the useful segment.** In the trimming view, exclude the time spent opening apps or navigating away. Tap **Start Stitching** to process the selected content.
6. **Review and save.** Inspect the long image, adjust joins when necessary, and save the finished result to Photos.

If you already have a scrolling screen recording in your library, choose **Select Video to Stitch** on the Auto Stitch screen. Import it, trim the relevant portion, and continue with stitching.

You still control the scrolling. ScrollShot analyzes the captured frames, finds overlapping content, and combines it into a long image. A steady recording gives it better source material than fast swipes through changing content.

### Option B: Stitch existing screenshots

For a shorter conversation or screenshots you have already taken, Manual Stitch gives you direct control over the sequence.

![ScrollShot Manual Stitch album browser on iPhone Duo with a grid of images and the Recents album selector](/scrollshot_images_iphone_duo_en.webp)

*Browse your album in Manual Stitch, then select overlapping screenshots in reading order. The example shows the album browser before selection.*

1. Capture the first section, then scroll down partway before taking the next screenshot.
2. Leave several lines or a recognizable block of content visible in both neighboring images. That overlap helps the app identify where they join.
3. Open **Manual Stitch** in ScrollShot and allow access to the screenshots you want to use.
4. Select them from top to bottom in reading order. Check the selection numbers before continuing.
5. Choose **Long Shot** for overlapping screenshots, then inspect the stitched preview and save it.

By default, ScrollShot keeps your selection order. If you have enabled **Manual Stitch Sort** in Settings, it sorts by creation time instead. Check that setting if the preview begins with the wrong image.

Avoid mixing screenshots with different text sizes, zoom levels, or display layouts. Even if they show the same page, the lines may wrap differently and no longer match.

## Tips for cleaner captures on iPhone Duo

**Choose your layout before starting.** Decide whether to use the outer or inner display, then keep the same orientation and app width throughout the capture. As a practical stitching precaution, avoid folding, unfolding, or resizing the app midway through a recording.

**Capture one readable section at a time.** A single image containing an entire lengthy conversation can become inconvenient to read or send. Separate it at natural topic breaks if necessary.

**Check the source before repeating the stitch.** Missing content usually needs a better recording or another screenshot. Fine-tuning cannot restore text that was never captured. If a join looks wrong, first check for a gap between the source images.

**Review the shared copy.** Some messaging services shrink large images. Open what you sent and check the text; use a file attachment or PDF if that better preserves readability for the recipient.

Yes. Use Safari's Full Page option for supported webpages. For content that does not offer Full Page, use ScrollShot to stitch a scrolling recording or overlapping screenshots.

### Can I save a long screenshot as an image instead of a PDF?

Yes. Choose Save to Photos when available in the screenshot editor. ScrollShot also saves stitched images to Photos and supports sharing as PDF.

### Should I fold or unfold iPhone Duo while recording?

Choose your display layout before recording and keep it consistent. If the content changes width or rearranges during capture, start a new recording for that layout.
